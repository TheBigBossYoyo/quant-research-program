GENERAL MECHANISM (confirmed from the isin.org explanatory page and corroborated by the WisdomTree corporate-action notice titles found): WisdomTree/ETFS periodically performs SHARE CONSOLIDATIONS (reverse splits) on some of its ETC lines - most commonly on LEVERAGED daily products whose NAV has decayed toward a level the issuer considers operationally inconvenient (e.g. sub-GBP-1 or sub-cent unit prices), but the mechanism is generic and could in principle apply to any share class. A consolidation changes the ISIN of the affected security: "Consolidations result in a change of ISINs for the Affected Securities. Trading of the consolidated Affected Securities under their old ISINs ends on the record date, after which trading of the consolidated Affected Securities under their new ISINs begins."

SPECIFIC EXAMPLE FOUND (LEVERAGED product, outside this audit's unleveraged universe, but illustrating the mechanism issuer-wide): WisdomTree's Silver 3x Daily Leveraged ETC underwent a 10-for-1 reverse split, reported May 2026, attributed by the secondary source to a "structural deficit" in the leveraged product's NAV trajectory.

A separate December 2022 corporate action (title only, not read in full) references splits across several LEVERAGED collateralised ETC classes (WisdomTree WTI Crude 3x Daily Leveraged, WisdomTree S&P 500 3x Daily Leveraged, WisdomTree Brent Crude Oil 3x Daily Leveraged, WisdomTree Palladium 2x Daily Leveraged) - again, all leveraged products, not the unleveraged single-commodity ETCs that are this audit's actual candidate universe.

RELEVANCE TO THIS AUDIT'S UNLEVERAGED UNIVERSE: No confirmed example of an ISIN-changing SHARE CONSOLIDATION (as opposed to the separate ISIN-REISSUANCE pattern noted for Natural Gas/Lean Hogs in survivorship_wisdomtree_etfs_rebrand_2018.txt) was found this session specifically affecting an UNLEVERAGED (1x) single-commodity ETC. The mechanism is documented and real for the WisdomTree/ETFS ETC family generally (leverage decay makes it far more common on leveraged products), and the audit brief's own premise ("some WisdomTree ETCs had share consolidations - find examples") is therefore CONFIRMED AS A REAL, DOCUMENTED PHENOMENON, but the concrete examples found this session are all leveraged products. This distinction matters for E: a data pipeline that keys purely on today's ISIN for an UNLEVERAGED product is less likely (based on evidence found this session) to be broken by a consolidation-driven ISIN change than one for a leveraged product - but the SEPARATE Natural-Gas/Lean-Hogs-style reissuance pattern found elsewhere in this audit shows unleveraged products are not entirely immune to ISIN discontinuities either.
