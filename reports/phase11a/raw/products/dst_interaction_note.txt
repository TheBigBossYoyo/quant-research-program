ANALYTICAL NOTE (not a single-source excerpt) - DST interaction between US and EU/UK clocks
RETRIEVAL DATE: 2026-09-26 (compiled from general public knowledge of DST transition rules, cross-checked against the exchange-hours sources in this same directory)

FACTS (as stated in the audit brief and confirmed general knowledge, not from a single fetched page):
- US DST ("Daylight Saving Time") begins the second Sunday of March and ends the first Sunday of November.
- EU/UK DST ("British Summer Time" / "Central European Summer Time") begins the last Sunday of March and ends the last Sunday of October.

IMPLICATION FOR THIS STRATEGY: Because the US and EU/UK DST transition dates do NOT coincide (there are two short asymmetric windows each year - roughly early-to-mid March, and late October to early November - where US clocks have shifted but EU/UK clocks have not, or vice versa), the wall-clock offset between US Eastern time (where CFTC data is released and NYMEX/CBOT/COMEX/ICE-US trading occurs) and UK/CET time (where LSE/Xetra/Borsa Italiana trading occurs) is NOT constant year-round: it is 5 hours (US Eastern to UK) / 6 hours (US Eastern to CET) for most of the year, but temporarily changes by one hour during those asymmetric windows. Any execution-timing rule for a CFTC-driven European-ETC strategy (e.g. "place order N minutes after Friday's CFTC Commitments of Traders release, which itself has its own publication-delay conventions") must account for this asymmetry explicitly in code (comparing calendar dates against both DST rule sets) rather than assuming a fixed UTC offset - this is a pure calendar-logic point, not a data point requiring a source citation beyond the well-established DST transition rules themselves.
