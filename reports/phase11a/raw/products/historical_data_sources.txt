1. ISSUER-PUBLISHED NAV/PRICE HISTORY
   - WisdomTree (wisdomtree.eu / dataspanapi.wisdomtree.com factsheet and KID endpoints): issuer product pages typically expose downloadable factsheets and KIDs per ISIN; direct WebFetch access to the main wisdomtree.eu marketing pages returned HTTP 403 in this session (likely bot-protection), but the dataspanapi.wisdomtree.com PDF endpoints (FACTSHEET, PRIIP_KID) were reachable via search-engine indexing. Factsheets commonly include a short NAV-history chart/table; whether a full downloadable CSV/time-series export is offered was NOT independently confirmed this session (not tested, to comply with the metadata-only hard rule - no such download was attempted or opened).
   - iShares (ishares.com): iShares product pages are well-known industry-wide to offer a "Performance & literature" or "Price/Performance" tab with downloadable historical NAV data per share class; this is stated here as a general, well-established fact about the iShares platform rather than something independently re-confirmed by opening a specific download this session (again, to comply with the metadata-only hard rule, no NAV file was fetched or inspected).
   - Xtrackers/DWS (etf.dws.com): the DWS ETC product pages returned mostly a legal disclaimer/gateway page to WebFetch in this session ("You are about to leave the Xtrackers ETF website..."); whether a NAV-history download exists behind that gateway was not tested.
   - Deutsche Boerse Commodities (xetra-gold.com): being a bearer-bond-style product rather than a fund, Xetra-Gold's own site is oriented around the KID and legal documents; historical price series for 4GLD are more commonly obtained via the Xetra/Deutsche Boerse market-data channels described below rather than an issuer NAV file, since there is no "NAV" in the fund sense - only a market price for the bearer note.

2. EXCHANGE-SOLD HISTORICAL DATA

Commercial/paid.
   - Deutsche Boerse / Xetra: sells historical cash-market data via its market-data-and-analytics business (part of the same group as the Trading Calendar pages already fetched in this audit); exact product name/pricing for ETC-level EOD history was not obtained this session.
   - Borsa Italiana: as part of Euronext/LSEG-related market-data offerings; specific ETC-history product page not independently checked this session.

3. THIRD-PARTY VENDORS CARRYING LSE/XETRA ETC HISTORIES
   - EOD Historical Data (EODHD): per its own marketing copy, "procures end-of-day and delayed data through direct contracts with various exchanges, including LSE for UK data and European markets through Cboe, encompassing over 60 exchanges," and documents ETFs/mutual funds and international exchange coverage including LSE and Deutsche Boerse/Xetra using a SYMBOL.EXCHANGE ticker convention (e.g. "SYMBOL.LSE", "SYMBOL.XETRA"). This is the SAME provider already under an active subscription for this research program per project memory (eodhd-subscription-facts.md) - i.e. if European commodity-ETC history is ever needed for actual backtesting (a LATER, separate research decision, not authorised or attempted by this metadata audit), the existing EODHD subscription is a first port of call, subject to verifying its specific ETC coverage/depth/point-in-time integrity for the exact ISINs in this audit's product table, which was NOT done this session.
   - Refinitiv/LSEG (Eikon/Workspace, Tick History): institutional-grade, paid; "real-time, delayed, and end-of-day equity pricing across global markets... 500+ exchanges, 7 million+ instruments, and Tick History... tick-level historical data back to 1996 across 575+ global venues" per its own marketing copy. Certainly covers LSE/Xetra-listed ETCs at an institutional level; cost/access likely well beyond this project's stated EUR 500-1,000 capital scale and is listed here for completeness, not as a realistic near-term data source for this project.
   - Bloomberg: industry-standard terminal/data-license coverage of LSE/Xetra ETCs; not independently re-confirmed this session beyond general market knowledge; cost-prohibitive for this project's scale.

4. KNOWN DATA-QUALITY ISSUES FOR THIS PRODUCT CLASS (compiled from general knowledge of the ETC/ETP data-vendor landscape, cross-referenced against this session's own findings where applicable - NOT all individually re-verified against a vendor's own documentation this session):
   - Raw vs adjusted close: ETCs generally do NOT pay distributions (they are accumulating/roll-yield-embedded structures), so the raw-vs-adjusted-close distinction that matters for dividend-paying equities/ETFs is LESS material for this product class - but it is NOT irrelevant, because...
   - ...Share/unit CONSOLIDATIONS (reverse splits) DO occur on this product class (confirmed this session for leveraged WisdomTree ETC lines - see survivorship_share_consolidations_and_isin_reissuance.txt) and DO require a price adjustment factor exactly like a stock split would, and ALSO typically come with an ISIN change, meaning a naive vendor feed keyed only on today's ISIN will NOT automatically show a continuous pre/post-consolidation series without deliberate vendor-side splicing.
   - Distributions: ETCs are generally non-distributing (accumulating), so this is a smaller data-quality concern than for equities, but should still be confirmed per-product from the KID (some UCITS ETF-wrapper broad-commodity funds, as opposed to pure ETC bearer-note/collateral structures, could in principle have a distributing share class - not confirmed either way this session for any specific product in the table).
   - Delisted-product preservation: this audit's own task-C findings (WisdomTree/ETFS 2018-2019 ~192-product closure; leveraged-oil compulsory redemptions 2020; db x-trackers 2017 closures) demonstrate that a meaningful fraction of the 2006-2012-era product universe no longer exists under its original name/ISIN, so any vendor or self-built dataset must explicitly ask whether delisted/terminated tickers are retained in history (point-in-time integrity) rather than silently dropped - this was flagged as a general risk, consistent with lessons already recorded elsewhere in this research program's memory about survivorship bias, but was NOT tested against any specific vendor's actual delisted-ticker retention policy this session.
   - Currency lines: several products in this audit's table exist in multiple currency-denominated lines (e.g. WisdomTree Physical Gold PHAU-USD vs PHGP-GBP; various EUR-hedged share classes across issuers) under DIFFERENT ISINs for what is economically "the same" commodity exposure - a naive universe-construction step that treats each ISIN as an independent instrument without recognising these currency-hedge relationships could inadvertently multiple-count the same underlying commodity bet, inflating apparent diversification.
   - Stale/thin pricing and bid/ask spread availability: Xetra publishes execution-quality/liquidity metrics under its "Xetra Liquidity Measure" (XLM) framework (general market knowledge; not independently re-fetched this session) which can proxy for typical spread costs per instrument; LSE and issuers themselves (WisdomTree, iShares, Invesco all publish "indicative spread" or "average spread" figures in some factsheets per general market practice) are the other common sources of spread information - NEITHER was independently opened/verified this session (again, to avoid inadvertently capturing performance-adjacent figures under the metadata-only hard rule, since spread/liquidity figures sit close to, though are analytically distinct from, "performance").
