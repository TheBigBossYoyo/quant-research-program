**Phase 11B Stage 0 — literature + data audit only. Not a backtest. No price/return/flow time series were downloaded or computed.**
Retrieval date for all web/API evidence below: **2026-09-26**.
Local paper snapshots (where obtained) live in `reports/phase11b/raw/literature/` (git-ignored except `SOURCES.txt`).

Evidence tags used throughout: **VERIFIED_FULLTEXT** (I read the primary document myself, via PyMuPDF or direct fetch of the vendor's own page), **VERIFIED_DOC** (I fetched the vendor/official doc/API myself and quote it directly), **ABSTRACT_ONLY** (official abstract/metadata verified, body not read), **SECONDARY** (a third party — review site, blog, or an AI web-search/fetch summary — describing the primary source; not independently verified by me), **UNVERIFIED** (claim found but I could not corroborate it and am flagging it as possibly unreliable).

A methodological note up front: several `WebSearch` tool auto-summaries produced **wrong or unsourceable claims** during this audit (e.g. attributing Chainalysis entity labels to Makarov & Schoar 2021, when the primary text says the opposite — see §A.2 — and several specific "accuracy/correlation" statistics for whale-transaction models that I could not trace to any named paper). I do not repeat those unsourced numbers below. This is itself a data point for Part B: even literature triage is vulnerable to silent retroactive/low-fidelity "knowledge," which is the same failure mode this audit is checking for in wallet labels.

### Per-paper detail

**A.1 — Hoang, L.T. & Baur, D.G. (2022), "Loaded for bear: Bitcoin private wallets, exchange reserves and prices," *Journal of Banking & Finance* 144, 106622.**
DOI `10.1016/j.jbankfin.2022.106622` — **VERIFIED_DOC** (Crossref API metadata, retrieved 2026-09-26). Journal, volume, article number and Nov-2022 publication date confirmed this way.
I could not obtain the full text (ScienceDirect and SSRN both returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch, and no open-access PDF is listed by Semantic Scholar/Unpaywall — `is_oa: true` but `url_for_pdf: null`, i.e. the only "open" location is the paywalled SSRN landing page itself). Everything below this line for A.1 is **SECONDARY**, reconstructed from (a) the RePEc/IDEAS abstract page (official abstract, JEL codes, keywords — this part is closer to VERIFIED_DOC since I read the abstract page directly), and (b) AI-generated summaries of the SSRN/ScienceDirect/Quantpedia pages, which is a weaker source and I flag it as such.
- Abstract (verified via direct fetch of the RePEc page): *"bitcoin exchange reserve changes ... are negatively related to contemporaneous and future bitcoin returns"* and *"[t]ransfer of bitcoin on exchanges implies increased selling pressure."*
- Sample period **1,979 trading days, 2016-01-01 to 2021-06-01** — SECONDARY (repeated identically across two independent AI summaries of SSRN/UWA-repository pages, so I treat this specific number with moderate confidence, but it is not something I read in the primary text myself).
- Data source: one summary quotes what purports to be the paper's own text — *"the data vendor identifies addresses of various exchanges on the bitcoin blockchain and tracks the amount ... transferred"* — and separately names CryptoQuant, described as compiling reserves for "20 leading cryptocurrency exchanges." A **different** AI-search summary, earlier in this same session, instead claimed the authors "manually extracted sample addresses of known exchanges from the walletexplorer page." These two claims are not necessarily mutually exclusive (CryptoQuant could be the primary reserve series with WalletExplorer used for a validation subsample), but I cannot verify either claim against the primary text, so **I flag the exact data-vendor attribution for this paper as UNVERIFIED** — readers should not treat "CryptoQuant" as confirmed for this specific paper without pulling the actual PDF (a university library proxy would be the fastest route; I have no such access here).
- **No source I could reach discusses label vintage / point-in-time issues for this paper at all** — a silence that is itself notable, since this is exactly the paper whose central variable (exchange reserves) is most exposed to the retroactive-relabeling problem this audit exists to catch.

**A.2 — Makarov, I. & Schoar, A. (2021), "Blockchain Analysis of the Bitcoin Market," NBER Working Paper 29396.**
**VERIFIED_FULLTEXT** — downloaded directly from `nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29396/w29396.pdf` (3.0MB, 55 pages) and read in full with PyMuPDF. Local file: `makarov_schoar_nber_w29396.pdf`.
This is **not a return-prediction paper** — I searched the full extracted text for "return"/"predict"/"forecast" and found no return-prediction regressions anywhere; it is a purely descriptive/structural analysis of blockchain network participants (miner concentration, exchange similarity clustering, illicit-activity flows). I include it because it is the standard citation for on-chain entity identification methodology and because an earlier `WebSearch` auto-summary in this session **incorrectly** stated it uses "Chainalysis entity labels" — the primary text directly contradicts this:
- *"To link address clusters to real entities we scrape cryptocurrency blogs and websites"* (p.10 of the PDF) — Reddit, Blockchain.info, bitinfocharts.com, bitcointalk.org, **walletexplorer.com**, and Matbea.com, *"supplement[ed]... with the state-of-the-art database of crypto entities from Bitfury Crystal Blockchain"* — an AML/analytics vendor, not Chainalysis.
- Clustering: common-input-ownership heuristic; *"the original database has 896 million"* addresses (p.12), reduced to 189 million clusters.
- Directly on the point-in-time problem (p.13): *"the identification of entities is incomplete almost by design, since it relies on an entity either voluntarily disclosing its addresses or learning about"* an entity's addresses through interaction with it. This is the clearest first-hand academic statement I found anywhere in this audit that exchange/entity identification is inherently a **progressive discovery process** — precisely the mechanism that makes retroactive relabeling possible.
- They identify 1,043 distinct entities (393 exchanges, 86 gambling sites, etc.) and, separately, 4,507 "Likely Exchange/OTC/Trading-Desk" (LEOTD) clusters inferred purely from **flow patterns** (≥50% inflow from known exchanges, ≥40% outflow to known exchanges) rather than direct identification — an explicit acknowledgment that a meaningful share of "exchange" volume in any such study is inferred, not confirmed.
- Sample: entity-level monthly volumes plotted "from the beginning of 2015 until May 2021" (Figure 3).

**A.3 — Griffin, J.M. & Shams, A. (2020), "Is Bitcoin Really Untethered?," *The Journal of Finance* 75(4), 1913–1964.**
DOI `10.1111/jofi.12903` — **ABSTRACT_ONLY/VERIFIED_DOC** for bibliographic metadata (Semantic Scholar Graph API, retrieved 2026-09-26; the paper's own abstract text matches across SSRN/Wiley/RePEc records).
The Wiley "open access" PDF link surfaced by Semantic Scholar returned HTTP 403 to a direct fetch, and SSRN also returned 403. However, a full-text HTML render of this paper (obtained by a separate concurrent research session working in this same shared `reports/phase11b/raw/literature/` directory, filename `GriffinShams_2020_JoF_fulltext_htmlrender.txt`) was available in the shared folder; I read it directly myself, so I treat the following as **VERIFIED_FULLTEXT**:
- Mechanism: Tether ("USDT") flows from Bitfinex to Poloniex/Bittrex, with Bitcoin flowing back to Bitfinex, are followed by predictably higher BTC returns over the next ~3 hours. *"[T]he top 1% of hours with the largest lagged combined Bitcoin and Tether net flows... are associated with 58.8% of Bitcoin's compounded return"* over the March 2017–March 2018 window.
- Address identification (Bitcoin side): common-input clustering (their own), then *"the addresses of a number of wallets belonging to Tether exchanges are collected from public forums and individual investors who transferred Bitcoin to these exchanges."* **No date-stamping discipline is described for this step.**
- Address identification (Tether side): *"wallet identities of major exchanges are manually collected from the Tether rich list on tether.to at **all snapshots available on Internet Archive**"* — this part of their methodology **is** point-in-time-conscious (using dated Wayback Machine captures), in contrast to the Bitcoin-side identification.
- The flow is attributed to a single large account/entity at Bitfinex, so this is closer to a **manipulation/single-actor case study** than a generalizable, tradeable "exchange reserve" signal in the Hoang & Baur sense — a genuinely different flavor of "exchange flow predicts price" evidence, worth keeping analytically separate from netflow/reserve-level studies.
- No transaction costs modeled; robustness via 10,000-simulation bootstrap and placebo tests on non-Bitfinex exchange pairs.

**A.4 — Chi, Y., Chu, Q.(R.) & Hao, W. (2025), "Return and Volatility Forecasting Using On-Chain Flows in Cryptocurrency Markets," working paper (Univ. of Auckland / NTU / Univ. of Leicester), arXiv 2411.06327.**
**VERIFIED_FULLTEXT** — downloaded and read in full (29 pages) with PyMuPDF. Local file: `onchain_flow_forecasting_arxiv_2411.06327.pdf`.
- Abstract (p.1): *"ETH net inflows to strongly [and negatively] predict ETH returns and volatility... BTC net inflows generally lack predictive power on BTC returns (except at 4 hours)... USDT flowing... into cryptocurrency exchanges... positively predicts BTC and ETH returns."*
- Sample: 2017-12-16 to 2023-01-20 (p.3). Data sourced across a named list of ~20 exchanges (Binance, Bitfinex, Bitget, Bithumb, BitMEX, Bitstamp, Bittrex, Bybit, etc., footnote 2, p.3) — **no vendor name (Glassnode/CryptoQuant/Kaiko/etc.) or address-clustering methodology is disclosed anywhere in the text I read**, and I searched specifically for "clustering," "provider," "vendor" and found nothing. This is a meaningful gap for any downstream user trying to assess label vintage.
- Regressions of form R(t+1) = β0 + β1·NetInflow(t) [+ β2·R(t)] for 1/2/3/4/6-hour horizons (pp.3-4), reported with explicit "in-sample and out-of-sample regression tests" (p.1) — though the paper does not describe a walk-forward/expanding-window OOS scheme in the pages I read; "OOS" appears to refer to a held-out subsample rather than a rolling backtest.
- Table 1 (p.20) heatmap, e.g. 1-hour single-variable ETH regression: net-inflow coefficient **−0.017 (p<0.01)** on ETH returns and **−0.80 (p<0.01)** on ETH volatility; USDT net-inflow coefficient on ETH returns **+1.1×10⁻⁵ (p<0.01)**.
- A costed ETH options overlay (Section 2.4-2.5, pp.4-6) models call-option premium (0.03% of index price), perpetual-hedge cost (0.05%×delta), and a bid-ask-spread term — genuinely cost-aware, unusually so for this literature — but this cost model is layered on the *predictive signal*, not on a direct spot/perp exchange-reserve trading rule.

**A.5 — Herremans, D. & Low, K.W. (2022), "Forecasting Bitcoin volatility spikes from whale transactions and CryptoQuant data using Synthesizer Transformer models," arXiv 2211.08281 (→ *Expert Systems with Applications*).**
**VERIFIED_FULLTEXT** for the abstract/introduction (pp.1-4 read directly); local file `whale_transactions_cryptoquant_arxiv_2211.08281.pdf` (39 pages; remainder not read in depth).
- *"we gather a dataset from CryptoQuant... as well as whale transaction tweets from January 2018 to September 2021"* (p.4) — CryptoQuant is named explicitly and directly as the commercial exchange/miner-flow data vendor, corroborating that CryptoQuant is a real, widely-used source in this literature (relevant cross-check for Part B).
- Target is **next-day volatility spike classification**, not return direction — complementary to, not a direct substitute for, the return-prediction papers above.
- No discussion of CryptoQuant's labeling methodology or data-revision policy found in the sections read.

**A.6 — Magner, N. & Sanhueza, A. (2025), "The Moby Dick effect: Contagious Bitcoin whales in the crypto market," *Finance Research Letters* 85 (2025).**
**SECONDARY** only — I could not obtain the PDF (ScienceDirect paywalled; no OA copy located); reconstructed from a WebSearch summary of the RePEc listing and ScienceDirect abstract page.
- Signal: individual whale transfers to/from exchanges as reported by the Whale Alert Telegram feed (an **event-level** signal, not an aggregated reserve/netflow time series).
- Method: time-varying-parameter VAR, contagion measured on the 15 largest-cap cryptocurrencies at 1h/6h/24h post-alert.
- Finding, per the secondary summary: significant contagion mainly at 6h/24h, and *"more pronounced for altcoins... than for Bitcoin itself"* — i.e., BTC's **own** price reacts less to BTC whale exchange transfers than other assets do, which sits in tension with a simple "BTC exchange inflow ⇒ BTC sell-off" reading and instead points to a cross-asset liquidity/sentiment contagion channel.

**A.7 — Anastasopoulos, A., Gradojevic, N., Liu, F., Maynard, A. & Tsiakas, I. (Nov. 2024 draft; EFMA 2025), "Order Flow and Cryptocurrency Returns," SSRN 5020002.**
**VERIFIED_FULLTEXT** for pp.1-5 (of 51), read directly; local file `anastasopoulos_et_al_order_flow_efma2025.pdf`.
Flagged deliberately as **adjacent-but-distinct**: *"[o]rder flow is a measure of the net demand... defined as the value of buyer-initiated transactions minus the value of seller-initiated transactions"* across **11 fiat-currency-denominated trading venues**, aggregated into "world order flow" — this is a **trade-classification (Lee-Ready-style) microstructure measure**, not an on-chain wallet-to-exchange transfer / reserve metric. It does not use exchange wallet labels at all, so the PIT wallet-label problem in Part B does not apply to it, but readers of this audit should not conflate its (positive, "permanent-view") findings with the exchange-reserve/netflow literature above.
- Sample: 82 cryptocurrencies, 2018-01-01 to 2022-06-30, daily & weekly.
- IS: contemporaneous world-order-flow regressions explain "about 11% of daily returns and 20% of weekly returns" (p.4-5). Predictive regressions orthogonalize lagged order flow against lagged returns and find a positive, significant relation with future returns — the paper's "permanent view."
- OOS: 9 ML model classes (ridge, LASSO, elastic net, PCR, random forest, gradient boosting, 4-layer NNs) for one-day-ahead prediction; order flow said to dominate fundamentals-only benchmarks out of sample.

## PART B — Point-in-time wallet-label audit, vendor by vendor

**Standard applied:** a source is **PIT_CLEAN** only if a researcher today can reconstruct, for any past date *t*, exactly the set of address→entity labels that were knowable as of *t* — with no metric ever silently recomputed using knowledge discovered after *t*. **PARTIAL_PIT** means this is possible only for a bounded sub-period, sub-universe, or via a specific opt-in product. **NOT_PIT** means the standard product is known to be retroactively mutable and no PIT alternative was found. **UNKNOWN** means I found no vendor documentation addressing the question either way.

### Key evidence, quoted directly

- **Glassnode**, "Introducing Point-in-Time Data" (Mar 15 2023; VERIFIED_DOC via `research.glassnode.com/introducing-point-in-time-data`): *"one has to expect information leakage from future clustering knowledge into data of old timestamps"* and *"[m]etrics may sometimes require a recomputation of their full historic data."* Glassnode's own PIT docs (VERIFIED_DOC, `docs.glassnode.com/data/point-in-time-metrics`) add the critical caveat that *"[a]ny metrics that had PIT enabled in July 2025 will not contain historical PIT data prior to that date"* — i.e., subscribing today does not retroactively grant clean history back to 2019.
- **Coin Metrics**, "Point-in-Time Flows" (VERIFIED_DOC, `gitbook-docs.coinmetrics.io/.../point-in-time-flows`): standard flows have *"past values [that] can change when new entity addresses are discovered later,"* whereas PIT flows have *"past values [that] are fixed; newly discovered addresses affect only intervals on or after their discovery date."* This is the single clearest vendor articulation of the exact mechanism this audit is checking for.
- **WalletExplorer.com** founder, own site (VERIFIED_DOC, `walletexplorer.com/info`): *"Name database is NOT updated (except some very rare cases) since 2016"*; *"I joined Chainalysis.com... I can't disclose names publicly."*
- **Llanos, D.R. (2026), "Open Bitcoin Metrics," arXiv 2607.03124** (VERIFIED_FULLTEXT, downloaded and read in full, 200 pages; local file `open_bitcoin_metrics_arxiv_2607.03124.pdf`) — an independent, non-industry academic cross-check on exactly this question: *"entity-adjustment heuristics, address clustering rules... [create] a recurrent reproducibility problem"* (p.5). It rates Glassnode as *"Medium to low [auditability]... provider heuristics and low-level conventions are not fully reproducible"* and CryptoQuant as *"Medium to low... exact implementation conventions are not fully auditable"* (p.6-7 table), and Coin Metrics as *"Medium... the full reconstruction code is not generally open"* (p.6). This corroborates the vendor-side findings above from a fully independent source.
- A real, current restatement event: Coin Metrics *"corrected 60 Bitcoin ETF network-data series spanning 19 months"* (Feb 2025–Sept 2026), announced Sept 22 2026, with **no published before/after deltas or stated root cause** (SECONDARY, `cryptodaily.co.uk`, a trade-press writeup of a Coin Metrics status-page notice I did not access directly) — a live illustration that the mutability risk documented by vendors in the abstract is not hypothetical.

## Final answer

**Is there any route to a PIT-clean exchange-reserve/flow series for BTC or ETH covering a multi-year development window such as 2019-2024? No — not a complete one.**

1. The only two vendor products found anywhere in this audit that are explicitly, documentedly point-in-time (Glassnode PiT, Coin Metrics Point-in-Time Flows) only have genuine point-in-time history from **roughly 2023-2025 onward** (Glassnode: PIT tracking metadata from 2024, full-platform coverage from July 2025 with no retroactive backfill before a metric's own enablement date; Coin Metrics: exact start date for PIT history undisclosed, but the product itself post-dates the 2019-2024 window in question and its *standard* series were still being restated as recently as September 2026).
2. The cleanest **non-vendor** attribution — exchanges' own dated Proof-of-Reserves address disclosures — begins **only in November 2022** (Binance, OKX), i.e. after roughly two-thirds of a 2019-2024 window has already elapsed, and cannot be safely projected backward without reintroducing exactly the retroactive-knowledge problem this audit exists to prevent. Not all exchanges even publish addresses (Kraken's PoR is Merkle-root-only).
3. The only label sources that are genuinely frozen-in-time from an *early* date (WalletExplorer.com, and academic datasets built from it) are frozen at **~2016-2018**, Bitcoin-only, and have not been extended since — useful only for validating against a pre-2019 window, not for 2019-2024.
4. Every standard (non-PIT) commercial exchange-reserve/netflow product examined (Glassnode default metrics, CryptoQuant, Coin Metrics standard exchange flows) is either explicitly admitted by the vendor (Glassnode) or independently documented by an academic reproducibility audit (Llanos 2026) to undergo retroactive full-history recomputation as new addresses/clusters are discovered — meaning any 2019-2024 reserve/netflow chart pulled from these vendors **today** reflects 2026 labeling knowledge projected backward onto 2019-2024 timestamps.

**Conclusion for the research protocol: this mechanism should be classified `PIT_LABEL_BLOCKED` for a 2019-2024 BTC/ETH development window**, per the standard stated in the task brief. A narrower, later-starting variant (Nov 2022 onward, using PoR-disclosed addresses only, or a Coin-Metrics-PIT-Flows-only design starting whenever that product's history actually begins) would not be PIT-blocked, but would not satisfy the "multi-year development window covering 2019-2024" this project's chronological design calls for, and would face a much shorter, more recent, and non-i.i.d. (crypto-bull-market-only) sample.
